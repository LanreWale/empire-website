The 7 Study Methods That Actually Work

Here are the note-taking and study methods included in the pack. Each one is backed by cognitive science research.

01

Cornell Notes

Best for: Lectures and readings

Divide the page into notes, cues, and summary. Write notes during class, add cues after, and summarize at the end. The cues become your self-quiz questions.

02

Mind Mapping

Best for: Concept-heavy subjects

Start with a central idea and branch outward to related concepts. Visual connections improve memory retention by up to 32% compared to linear notes.

03

Outline Method

Best for: Structured content

Hierarchical notes with main topics, subtopics, and details. Best for subjects that organize naturally into clear categories (history, biology, business).

04

Active Recall

Best for: Exam preparation

Close your notes and write down everything you remember. Then check what you missed. This is the single most effective study technique identified by cognitive research.

05

The Feynman Technique

Best for: Complex concepts

Explain the concept out loud as if teaching it to a 12-year-old. When you get stuck, you've found your gap. Go back, fill the gap, and continue.

06

SQ3R Method

Best for: Textbook reading

Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. Turn headings into questions before reading. This converts passive reading into active processing.

07

Pomodoro Technique

Best for: Focus and consistency

Study in 25-minute blocks with 5-minute breaks. After 4 blocks, take a longer break. Prevents mental fatigue and maintains concentration.

Common GPA Mistakes

  • Retaking courses without checking the policy. Many schools replace the original grade; others average both. Check before retaking.
  • Ignoring credit hours. A 4-credit course impacts your GPA more than a 3-credit course.
  • Cramming for low-credit courses. Better to spend that time on high-credit courses where the impact is larger.
  • Ignoring pass/fail options. If a course is hard and not required, pass/fail preserves your GPA.
