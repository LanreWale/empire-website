Why Most Freelancers Undercharge

Most freelancers set their rate by guessing what feels fair or copying what they see on freelance platforms. This almost always leads to undercharging because it ignores the real cost of self-employment: self-employment tax (an additional 15.3% in the US), unpaid vacation and sick days, software subscriptions, equipment, and the 20–30% of your time spent on non-billable work like marketing, admin, and client communication.

A freelancer charging $40/hour and billing 25 hours per week earns $52,000/year gross — but after expenses, taxes, and unpaid time off, their take-home is closer to $32,000–$36,000. That's why this calculator factors in every cost so you know your true break-even rate and your target rate for the income you actually want.

How to Calculate Your Freelance Rate

Here's the exact formula professional freelancers use to set their rate. It's not a guess — it's math.

Step 1: Annual business expenses

Software, insurance, equipment, education. Example: $6,000/year

Step 2: Personal expenses and desired income

Rent, food, savings, entertainment. Example: $60,000/year

Step 3: Add self-employment tax

25–30% of income. Example: 30% × $60,000 = $18,000

Step 4: Total annual need

$6,000 + $60,000 + $18,000 = $84,000

Step 5: Estimate billable hours

Full-time work is 2,080 hours/year, but 20–30% is non-billable. Example: 1,400 billable hours

Step 6: Minimum hourly rate

$84,000 ÷ 1,400 = $60/hour

The critical insight: The $60/hour figure is your break-even rate. It's what you need to charge to cover your costs and hit your income target. Anything above it is profit. Most freelancers charge 20–40% below their break-even rate without realizing it.
