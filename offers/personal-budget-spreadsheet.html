The 50/30/20 Rule: A Simple Framework

Before you dive into 50+ categories, start with the 50/30/20 rule. It's the fastest way to sanity-check whether your budget is in the right ballpark.

Needs — 50%
50%
Wants — 30%
30%
Savings — 20%
20%

What counts as "Needs"

Rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation, minimum debt payments. These are expenses you cannot avoid without serious consequences.

What counts as "Wants"

Dining out, entertainment, streaming services, hobbies, vacations, gifts, upgrades. These are optional — reducing them doesn't threaten your stability.

What counts as "Savings"

Emergency fund, retirement contributions, investments, extra debt payments, sinking funds for future purchases. This is the category that builds wealth.

If your actual spending doesn't match this framework, that's the first clue where to adjust. Most people overspend on "wants" and underspend on "savings" without realizing it.

How To Use The Spreadsheet

Step 1: Enter Your Monthly Income

Type your take-home pay in the income row at the top. If you have multiple income sources (salary, side hustle, freelance), add each as a separate line. The spreadsheet automatically sums your total income.

Step 2: Fill In Your Expenses

Go through each category and enter what you spend. Don't worry about exact figures for the first month — estimates work fine. Check your bank and credit card statements from the last 30 days to get realistic numbers.

Step 3: Review The Dashboard

The summary dashboard at the top shows your total income, total expenses, remaining balance, and savings rate as a percentage. If you're spending more than you earn, the dashboard flags it immediately.

Step 4: Set Savings Goals

Navigate to the savings tracker tab. Enter your goal name, target amount, and target date. The tracker calculates how much you need to save each month and shows a progress bar that updates as you add savings entries.

Step 5: Plan Debt Payoff

Enter each debt's balance, interest rate, and minimum payment. The calculator shows your payoff timeline with minimum payments and with extra payments — so you can see exactly how much time and interest you save by paying a little extra each month.

Debt Payoff Strategy: Snowball vs. Avalanche

Two strategies dominate personal finance for paying off debt. The spreadsheet includes calculators for both. Here's how they compare with a real example:

The Setup

You have $500 extra per month to put toward debt. Your debts are:

  • Credit card: $3,000 balance, 22% APR, $90 minimum
  • Car loan: $8,000 balance, 6% APR, $250 minimum
  • Student loan: $15,000 balance, 5% APR, $200 minimum

Avalanche (Highest Interest First)

Pay minimums on everything, then throw all extra money at the credit card (highest APR). Once the credit card is gone, attack the car loan. Then the student loan.

Result: Debt-free in ~4 years, 9 months. Total interest paid: ~$2,900.

Snowball (Smallest Balance First)

Pay minimums on everything, then throw all extra money at the credit card (smallest balance). Same order here, but for different reasons — the momentum of eliminating a debt completely keeps you motivated.

Result: Debt-free in ~4 years, 9 months. Total interest paid: ~$2,940.

The Verdict

Mathematically, avalanche saves more money. Psychologically, snowball keeps more people on the plan. The difference in this example is only $40 — small enough that either approach works. Pick the one that keeps you motivated.

If your smallest balance has a much higher interest rate than your largest (e.g., a $500 credit card at 24% APR vs. a $15,000 student loan at 5%), avalanche saves significantly more.

The spreadsheet's debt calculator lets you test both scenarios with your actual numbers, so you can see exactly what each strategy would do in your situation.
