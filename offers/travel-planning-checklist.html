The 6-Week Pre-Departure Countdown

Start 4–6 weeks before departure for stress-free preparation. Here's the week-by-week breakdown.

6 Weeks Before — Book & Confirm

  • Book flights and accommodation
  • Apply for visas if needed
  • Check passport expiration (must be valid 6+ months past your return date)
  • Book any special experiences (tours, restaurants, shows) that fill up early

4 Weeks Before — Health & Insurance

  • Purchase travel insurance
  • Schedule any required vaccinations
  • Refill prescriptions for the full trip duration + extra
  • Research local health requirements for your destination
  • Download offline maps and translation apps

2 Weeks Before — Prep Home & Pack

  • Arrange house/pet sitters
  • Hold mail or redirect packages
  • Notify bank and credit card companies of travel dates
  • Start laying out items to pack
  • Buy any missing travel essentials now (not at the airport)

1 Week Before — Finalize

  • Print all confirmations and copies of documents
  • Pack suitcases (leave one outfit in carry-on)
  • Charge all electronics
  • Set up travel notifications on banking apps
  • Confirm transportation to/from airport

Day Before — Last Checks

  • Check in for your flight (24 hours in advance)
  • Confirm hotel check-in times
  • Set up out-of-office email
  • Empty fridge of perishables
  • Lock windows and doors
  • Set thermostat
  • Share final itinerary with someone you trust

Departure Day — Essentials Only

Phone, wallet, passport, boarding pass, medications, house keys, and a charged portable charger. That's all you truly need in your hands until you reach the airport.

Travel Insurance — What to Look For

Travel insurance isn't optional. Medical emergencies abroad can cost $10,000–$100,000+ without coverage. Here's the framework for choosing the right policy.

Medical$100,000+Hospital stays, doctor visits, emergency care

Common Exclusions

  • Pre-existing medical conditions (unless waived with a "cancel for any reason" upgrade)
  • Extreme sports and adventure activities (paragliding, scuba, etc.)
  • Travel to countries with active travel warnings
  • Alcohol or drug-related incidents
  • Intentional damage or negligence

How to File a Claim

  1. Document everything — take photos, keep receipts
  2. Get written statements from anyone involved (police, hotel, airline)
  3. Submit the claim within 30 days of the incident
  4. Include your policy number, proof of purchase, and supporting documents
  5. Follow up every 2 weeks until resolved

Smart Travel Tips From Frequent Travelers

The "One Outfit in Your Carry-On" Rule

Always pack one complete change of clothes in your carry-on bag. If your checked luggage is delayed (which happens on roughly 1 in 150 flights), you'll have something clean to wear while you sort things out. This is the single most common travel advice from experienced frequent flyers.

Local SIM vs International Roaming

In most countries, a local prepaid SIM card is 5–10x cheaper than international roaming. Research local SIM options before you arrive. Many airports have SIM card kiosks in the arrivals hall. Alternatively, eSIM services let you activate a local data plan before you land.

The Photo-First Insurance Claim

If you need to make a travel insurance claim — for lost luggage, delayed flights, or stolen items — photographic evidence dramatically speeds up the process. Photograph your packed luggage before departure, keep receipts for all purchases abroad, and screenshot any delay notifications from your airline.

Scan Everything Before You Go

Scan your passport, visa, insurance policy, and any important documents. Email the scans to yourself and save them in cloud storage. If your physical documents get lost or stolen, you'll have everything you need to get replacements quickly.

Split Your Money

Never keep all your cash and cards in one place. Split cash between a wallet, a money belt, and a hidden pocket in your luggage. Keep one credit card separate from the others. If you get pickpocketed, you won't lose everything at once.
